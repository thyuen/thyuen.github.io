My projects translate cryptographic research into practical technologies for trustworthy digital systems, with current work spanning blockchain privacy, digital finance, threshold cryptography, and post-quantum security.

## Current projects

### [Implications of Cryptocurrencies for the Pacific](https://research.monash.edu/en/projects/implications-of-cryptocurrencies-for-the-pacific/)

**Role:** Primary Chief Investigator  
**Period:** May 2026 – February 2027

This interdisciplinary project examines the implications of cryptocurrencies for Pacific Island countries. It considers how digital assets and related financial technologies may affect economies, institutions, communities, and policy development in the region.

### [NextGen Blockchain Privacy & Security: Practical and Quantum-Safe Solutions](https://research.monash.edu/en/projects/nextgen-blockchain-privacy-security-practical-and-quantum-safe-so/)

**Role:** Chief Investigator  
**Period:** January 2026 – January 2029  
**Funding:** Australian Research Council, A$557,398

This project develops practical and quantum-safe cryptographic tools for blockchain privacy and security. Its research includes confidential transactions, secure digital wallets, and privacy-preserving payment channels, aiming to improve the security, efficiency, and trustworthiness of digital payments.

### [Efficient Weighted Threshold Signature](https://research.monash.edu/en/projects/efficient-weighted-threshold-signature/)

**Role:** Chief Investigator  
**Period:** September 2025 – September 2026

This project investigates efficient weighted threshold-signature mechanisms, where participants can hold different signing weights. Such mechanisms support flexible and accountable joint authorisation for distributed digital systems.

## Completed projects

### [More Efficient and Scalable Post-Quantum Multi-Signatures](https://research.monash.edu/en/projects/more-efficient-and-scalable-post-quantum-multi-signatures/)

**Role:** Chief Investigator  
**Period:** August 2024 – December 2025

This project investigated more efficient and scalable multi-signature schemes designed for the post-quantum setting. It contributes to the development of cryptographic tools that can remain secure against future quantum-computing threats.
